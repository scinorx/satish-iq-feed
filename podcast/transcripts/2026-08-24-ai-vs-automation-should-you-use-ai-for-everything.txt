Hello, and welcome to In Simple Terms with Satish.

Ship-It Sam and Verify Veera are standing before two whiteboards.

They must prepare one thousand products for an online launch.

Sam has drawn one enormous box. Inside it, he has written: “AI agent.” Supplier documents enter the box. Prices, inventory settings, descriptions, and publication schedules come out.

Veera studies the diagram.

“Your AI is doing everything?”

“That is the idea.”

“Reading supplier documents?”

“Yes.”

“Creating descriptions, calculating prices, generating SKUs, and scheduling publication?”

“Yes.”

Veera pauses. “Is there anything it isn't doing?”

Sam smiles. “Taking credit for my architecture.”

Veera draws several smaller boxes.

Sam looks concerned. “That appears to be considerably more work.”

“Initially,” Veera says.

Sam points to his design. “My agent receives one objective, understands the context, and coordinates the workflow. If something changes, I update the instructions. Why build five automations?”

“That is a reasonable argument,” Veera replies. “I know AI can perform these steps. I am asking whether every step needs AI.”

Before Sam can answer, a supplier message arrives. The approved spreadsheet is gone. In its place are two photographs and one sentence:

“New bottle. Same as last year, but smaller and better for cold drinks.”

Veera's structured import cannot read it. Sam gives it to his AI agent.

The agent examines the photographs, finds last year's product, interprets the message, creates a structured record, and drafts a customer-friendly description.

Sam turns to Veera. “Still think the large AI box is unnecessary?”

“For this step, you were right. The information was unstructured, and the output required interpretation and creation.”

Sam pretends to photograph the moment.

Then the finance team sends another message. The margin for drinkware has changed from fifteen percent to twelve percent.

Sam updates his agent: “Find every drinkware product and recalculate its price.” The AI can perform the task.

Veera changes one value in her pricing rule. Her system identifies the affected products, recalculates their prices, and records the exact rule used.

Veera looks at Sam. “Still want AI calculating every known formula?”

Sam slowly lowers his imaginary camera. “For this step, you were right. The company has already decided the answer.”

“Exactly. Capability is not the same as suitability.”

Sam does not surrender. “Traditional automation also costs money. Someone must build the rules, maintain integrations, and update them when the process changes.”

“Correct,” Veera says. “AI is not automatically expensive, and automation is not automatically cheap. We compare the complete systems.”

She writes four questions:

How quickly can we build it?

How consistently does it operate?

What does it cost at scale?

How easily can we explain and change it?

“My approach gives us speed and adaptability,” Sam says.

“Especially while we are discovering the workflow,” Veera replies.

“And yours gives us repeatability when the process becomes stable.”

They return to the whiteboards.

Sam makes his AI box smaller—but more important.

AI reads inconsistent documents, interprets unclear information, and creates customer-friendly content.

Automation validates required fields, generates SKUs, applies pricing formulas, manages inventory, and schedules publication.

Before AI changes a price or publishes a product, deterministic validation checks its structured output.

Sam studies the design. “So AI provides interpretation and creativity.”

“And automation provides repeatability and control.”

“Which one won?”

Veera points to the connected boxes. “The system.”

Their conversation gives us a practical method.

Break a workflow into individual steps. If a rule and required output are already defined, begin with automation. If a step requires ambiguity, unstructured information, prediction, or generation, consider AI. Before a consequential action, add proportionate validation or human approval. Then measure quality, time, model calls, operating cost, and human intervention across the complete workflow.

Successful AI adoption is not measured by how many steps contain AI. It is measured by whether AI makes the complete system better.

Sam asked, “Can AI perform this task?” Veera asked, “Does this task need AI?” The strongest team asks both.
